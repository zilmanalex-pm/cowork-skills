# Business Phase Questionnaire — Template

Use this as the basis for generating a client intake questionnaire Word document (.docx). When asked to create a questionnaire, read this template and generate a polished, fillable Word document using the docx skill.

The questionnaire should be warm and direct in tone — not a corporate intake form. The client is a person, not a company. Frame questions to invite genuine reflection, not checkbox answers.

---

## Section 1 — Who You Are

1. Full name, as it should appear on the site
2. Credentials and licenses (e.g., M.A., Ph.D., license type, supervising body)
3. Years in practice
4. Theoretical approach(es) (e.g., CBT, psychodynamic, ACT, EMDR, somatic, integrative)
5. **In your own words: how do you describe yourself as a therapist?** *(Not credentials — character, presence, how people experience you. This is the most important question.)*
6. What drew you to this work?

---

## Section 2 — What You Do

1. Main areas of focus or specialization
2. Types of clients you work with (individuals, couples, teens, groups; age range, life stage)
3. Types of clients or situations you prefer not to take on
4. What a first session feels like with you (tone, structure, what a client might feel walking out)
5. What makes your approach different from what a client might find elsewhere

---

## Section 3 — Your Clients

1. **Who is your ideal client?** *(Describe as a person, not a diagnosis — who are they, what's going on in their life, what brings them to you?)*
2. What do your clients typically say when they first come to you (their words, not clinical terms)
3. What worries do people often have about starting therapy
4. What transformation do your clients typically experience
5. What feedback do you get most often from clients (about you specifically)

---

## Section 4 — Your Practice

1. Where you practice (city, neighborhood, clinic name)
2. In-person, online, or both
3. Languages you work in
4. Insurance accepted or private pay only
5. Standard session length
6. Whether to publish a fee range on the site
7. Preferred contact method for new clients (email, phone, WhatsApp, form)
8. Current availability (taking new clients, how many slots)

---

## Section 5 — The Website

1. Primary goal of the website (attract clients / credibility / both — rank them)
2. What you want someone to feel after 2 minutes on the site
3. Main action you want visitors to take
4. What would make you uncomfortable on the site
5. Websites you like the feel of (with links if possible)

---

## Section 6 — Tone and Look

1. Up to 5 words describing the atmosphere you want the site to convey
2. Warm-personal vs professional-formal — where on this scale
3. Color preferences, colors to avoid
4. Professional photo availability (or willingness to get one)

---

## Section 7 — Existing Assets

1. Existing bio or written description
2. Any articles, posts, or presentations about their work
3. Any phrase or paragraph they've written that felt truly accurate
4. Professional photos

---

## Section 8 — Anything Else

1. Anything important not covered above
2. Concerns or hesitations about having a public website
3. Timeline

---

## Generating the Document

When creating the Word document from this template:
- Use the `docx` skill
- Design with a calm, professional aesthetic (soft teal header accent, clean typography)
- Include answer lines/space after each question
- Add brief contextual hints in italics for the harder questions
- End with a warm closing note
- The document should feel like it was made specifically for them, not generated from a generic template
